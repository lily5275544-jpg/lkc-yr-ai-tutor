Case 1: Exertional Shortness of Breath and Systolic Murmur

A 78-year-old man presents with gradually worsening shortness of breath on exertion. He has no chest pain or syncope.

Examination

BP 138/72 mmHg, HR 68/min regular.
Apex beat is not displaced.
There is an ejection systolic murmur loudest at the right upper sternal border, radiating to both carotids.
S2 is soft and the carotid pulse is slow-rising.
There are fine bibasal crepitations.

Your Clinical Reasoning

What is the most likely diagnosis? Give the key findings supporting your answer.



Case 2: Difficulty Climbing Stairs

A patient presents with gradually increasing difficulty climbing stairs and getting up from a chair.

Examination

There is symmetrical proximal muscle weakness in both upper and lower limbs. Sensation is intact. There is no ptosis or fatigability and no upper motor neuron signs.

Your Clinical Reasoning

Where is the lesion most likely localised? What are your main differential diagnoses?
